be two Mori fiber spaces which are the output of a minimal model program for a kawamata log terminal pair $(W,B_W)$. Then $\phi'$ may be obtained from

\section{Introduction}
Let $(X,B)$ be a kawamata log terminal pair. The minimal model program 
predicts that there is a finite sequence of well understood
birational maps (known as $K_X+B$ flips and divisorial
contractions) inducing a rational map $f:X\dasharrow X'$
such that if $K_X+B$ is pseudo-effective, then $(X',f_*B)$ is a minimal model 
(i.e. $K_{X'}+f_*B'$ is nef)
and if $K_X+B$ is not pseudo-effective, then there is a Mori-fiber space
$\phi :X'\to S$ (such that $\rho (X'/S)=1$ and $-(K_{X'}+f_*B)$ is ample).
It is not the case that a given pair $(X,B)$ has a unique 
minimal model (resp. a unique Mori fiber space), however the minimal model 
program predicts that any two minimal models (resp. Mori fiber spaces)
should be related in a very precise manner (the terminalizations of two 
minimal models are related by a sequence of flops, resp.
two Mori fiber spaces are related 
by a sequence of Sarkisov links cf. \eqref{t-main}).
  
By \cite{BCHM}, the minimal model program is known to hold when $B$ or $K_X+B$ are big. It is then easy to see that the terminalizations of any two minimal 
models are connected by a sequence of flops.
Somewhat suprisingly, Kawamata \cite{Kawamata07} has shown that this holds 
in full generality (i.e. even when we do not assume that $B$ or $K_X+B$ are big). Note that
in this more general context, the existence of minimal models is still conjectural.

It is also shown (in full generality) 
in \cite{BCHM} that if $K_X+B$ is not pseudo-effective, 
then there is a Mori-fiber space $\phi :X'\to S$. It is then natural to expect 
that one can prove (in full generality) that any two Mori-fiber spaces
are related 
by a sequence of Sarkisov links.
The purpose of this paper is to show that this is indeed the case.
\begin{theorem} Let $(X,B)$ be a kawamata log terminal pair such that
$K_X+B$ is not pseudo-effective. Then any two corresponding Mori
fiber spaces are related by a sequence of Sarkisov links.
\end{theorem}
The proof is based on the original ideas of the Sarkisov program (as explained by Corti and Reid \cite{Corti95}).
The main twist is that we are unable to show termination of an arbitrary
sequence of flips, nor the acc property for pseudo-effective thresholds.
Instead our argument is based of the principle of
finiteness of minimal models for
kawamata log terminal
pairs $(Y,\Gamma )$ such that $\Gamma$ varies in a compact subset
of the big cone (cf. \cite{BCHM}).

\section{Notation and conventions}
\label{s-notation}
We work over the field of complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$.  A $\mathbb{R}$-Cartier divisor
$D$ on a normal variety $X$ is \textit{nef} if $D\cdot C\geq 0$ for any curve $C\subset X$. 
We say that two $\mathbb{R}$-divisors $D_1$, $D_2$ are $\mathbb{R}$-linearly
equivalent ($D_1\sim _{\mathbb{R}} D_2$) if $D_1-D_2=\sum r_i(f_i)$
where $r_i\in \R$ and $f_i$ are rational functions on $X$.
We say that a $\mathbb{R}$-Weil divisor $D$ is {\it big} 
if we may
find an ample $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $A$ and an effective $\mathbb{R}$-divisor $B$, such
that $D \sim _{\mathbb{R}} A+B$.
A divisor $D$ is {\it pseudo-effective}, 
if for any ample divisor $A$ and any rational number $\epsilon >0$, the
divisor $D+\epsilon A$ is big.
If $A$ is a $\mathbb Q$-divisor, we say that $A$ is a {\it general ample
divisor} if $A$ is ample and there is a sufficiently divisible integer $M>0$
such that $MA$ is very ample and $MA\in |MA|$ is very general.
If $A$ is a $\mathbb R$-divisor, we say that $A$ is a {\it general ample
divisor} if $A=\sum r_iA_i$ where $r_i\in \R$ and $A_i$ are general ample
$\mathbb Q$-divisors.

A \textit{log pair} $(X,\Delta)$ is a normal variety $X$ and an effective
$\mathbb{R}$-Weil divisor $\Delta$ such that $K_X+\Delta$ is 
$\mathbb{R}$-Cartier. 
 We say
that a log pair $(X,\Delta)$ is \textit{log smooth}, if $X$ is smooth and the support of
$\Delta$ is a divisor with global normal crossings.  A projective morphism $g \colon
Y\to X$ is a \textit{log resolution} of the pair $(X,\Delta )$ if $Y$ is smooth and
$g^{-1}(\Delta )\cup \{\,\text {exceptional set of $g$}\,\}$ is a divisor with normal
crossings support.  We write $g^*(K_X +\Delta )=K_Y +\Gamma$ and $\Gamma =\sum b_i\Gamma
_i$ where $\Gamma _i$ are distinct reduced irreducible divisors and $g_*\Gamma =\Delta$.  The discrepancy of
$\Gamma_i$ is $a(\Gamma _i,X,\Delta)=-b_i$.  

A pair $(X,\Delta)$ is {\it kawamata
log terminal} (KLT) if $b_i<1$ for all $i$.
We say that the pair
$(X,\Delta)$ is \textit{log canonical} if $b_i\leq 1$ for all $i$.
We say that the pair
$(X,\Delta)$ is \textit{terminal} if the discrepancy of any exceptional
divisor is greater than zero.

\section{Preliminaries}

\begin{lemma}\label{l-2} Let $(W,B_W)$ be a terminal pair, $(X,B_X)$ a log 
pair and $f:W\dasharrow X$ a birational map that extracts no 
divisors. If $K_X+B_X$ is nef and
$a(E,X,B_X)\geq a(E,W,B_{W})$ for all divisors $E\subset W$
then $a(E,X,B_X)\geq a(E,W,B_{W})$ for all divisors $E$ over $W$.
In particular \begin{enumerate}
\item$(X,B_X)$ is kawamata log terminal,
\item if $X'\to X$ is a divisorial blow up extracting a divisor $E'$,
with $a(E',X,B_X)\leq 0$ then $E'$ is a divisor contained in $W$, and 
\item if $X'\to X$ is a terminalization of $(X,B_X)$, then $f':W\dasharrow X'$ extracts
no divisors.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Let $Z$ be a common log resolution and $p:Z\to X$ and $q:Z\to W$
be the induced morphisms. Then we may write
$$q^*(K_W+B_W)=p^*(K_X+B_X)+F$$ where $$q_*F=\sum _{E\subset W}(a(E,X,B_X)-a(E,W,B_W))E\geq 0.$$ By the Negativity Lemma, it follows that
$F\geq 0$ and hence that $a(E,X,B_X)\geq a(E,W,B_{W})$ for all divisors $E$ over $W$. 

As $a(E,W,B_{W})>-1$ for all divisors $E$ over $W$, it follows 
immediately that $(X,B_X)$ is kawamata log terminal.

To see (2), it suffices to notice that 
as $(W,B_W)$ is terminal and $a(E',W,B_W)\leq a(E',X,B_X)\leq 0$, then
$E'$ is not exceptional over $W$. Similarly, a terminalization only extracts divisors $E'_i$ of
discrepancy $a(E'_i,X,B_X)\leq 0$.
\end{proof}
\begin{definition}\label{d-1} Let $(X,B_X)$ and $(W,B_W)$ two $\Q$-factorial log pairs and $f:W\dasharrow X$ a birational map that extracts no divisors. We will write
$(W,B_W)\geq (X,B_X)$ if 
$a(E,W,B_W)\leq a(E,X,B_X)$ for all divisors $E\subset W$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}Let $(X,B_X)$ and $(X',B_{X'})$ be kawamata log terminal pairs. Then $(X,B_X)$ and $(X',B_{X'})$ are Sarkisov related if there exists a 
kawamata log terminal pair $(W,B_W)$ such that $(X,B_X)$ and $(X',B_{X'})$ are
the output of a $(W,B_W)$-MMP.\end{definition}
Notice that if $(X,B_X)$ and $(X',B_{X'})$ are Sarkisov related, then
we may find a log smooth terminal pair
$(W',B_{W'})$ and morphisms $p:W'\to X$ and $q:W'\to X'$ such that $(X,B_X)$ and $(X',B_{X'})$ are
the output of a $(W',B_{W'})$-MMP.

\section{The main result}
\begin{theorem}\label{t-main} A birational map of $n$-dimensional
Sarkisov related kawamata log terminal pairs
$(X,B_X)$ and $(X',B_{X'})$
$$
\CD
X @.\overset\Phi\dashrightarrow @.X' \\
@V\phi VV @.@VV\phi' V \\
S @.@. S' \\
\endCD
$$
between a $K_X+B_X$-Mori fiber space and a $K_{X'}+B_{X'}$-Mori fiber space
is a composite of a finite sequence of birational maps given by the 
following 4
types of links.%\footnote{These links are simpler than the usual ones, there is no sequence of flips. Did I make a mistake? Do you have a reference for the 2 ray game? Links of type (I)

$$
\begin{matrix}
& &  Z &\dashrightarrow & X_1\\
& \swarrow &  & & \\
X & & & & \downarrow \\
\downarrow & & & & \\
S & & \leftarrow & & S_1 \\
\end{matrix}
$$
where $Z\dasharrow X_1$ is a sequence of flips,
$Z \rightarrow X$ is a extremal divisorial contraction, 
$X_1 \to S_1$ is a Mori fiber space and
$\rho(S_1/S) = 1$.

\vskip.1in

Links of type (II)

$$
\begin{matrix}
& & Z &\dasharrow & Z' & & \\
& \swarrow & & & & \searrow & \\
X & & & & & & X_1 \\
\downarrow & & & & & & \downarrow \\
S & & & \overset\sim \leftarrow & & & S_1 \\
\end{matrix}
$$
where $Z\dasharrow Z'$ is a sequence of flips,
$Z \rightarrow X$ and $Z' \rightarrow X_1$ are extremal
divisorial
contractions
and $X_1 \to S_1$ is a Mori fiber space.

\vskip.1in

Links of type (III) 

$$
\begin{matrix}
X & & \\
& \searrow & \\
\downarrow & & X_1 \\
& & \downarrow \\
S &  \rightarrow  & S_1 \\
\end{matrix}
$$
where 
$X \rightarrow X_1$ is an extremal divisorial contraction,
$X_1 \to S_1$ is a Mori fiber space
and
$\rho(S/S_1) = 1$.

\vskip.1in

Links of type (IV)

$$
\begin{matrix}
X & & \dashrightarrow & & X_1 \\
\downarrow & & & & \downarrow \\
S & & & & S_1 \\
& \searrow & & \swarrow & \\
& & T & & \\
\end{matrix}
$$
where  $X \dashrightarrow X'$ is the identity (case IVa) or a log flip
(case IVb), 
$X_1 \to S_1$ is a Mori fiber space and $\rho(S/T) = \rho(S_1/T) = 1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Let $H'\sim_\R-(K_{X'}+B_{X'})+(\phi ')^*A'$, where $A'$ is an 
ample $\R$-divisor on $S'$ which is very general in ${\rm NS}_\R(S')$ 
and $H'$ is a very general very ample $\R$-divisor. 
Therefore, $K_{X'}+B_{X'}+H'\sim_\R (\phi ')^*A'$ is nef and kawamata log terminal.
Similarly, let $C$ be a very general very ample $\Q$-divisor on $X$
such that $K_X+B_X+C$ is nef, kawamata log terminal and
$K_X+B_X+C \sim _{\R}\phi ^* A$ where $A$ is an 
ample $\R$-divisor on $S$ which is very general in ${\rm NS}_\R(S)$.
We may assume that $(W,B_W)$ is log smooth, $p:W\to X$ and $q:W\to X'$ are morphisms and $B_W+p^*C+q^*H'+{\rm Ex}(p)+{\rm Ex}(q)$ has simple normal crossings.
We let $C_W=(p^{-1})_*C=p^*C$ and $H_W=(q^{-1})_*H=q^*H$, then we may assume that 
$(W,B_W+cC_W+hH_W)$ is terminal for any $0\leq c,h\leq 2$.


\begin{claim}\label{c-1} There exits a finite sequence of links $X_i\dasharrow X_{i+1}$
for $0\leq i\leq N$ such that $X=X_0$, $h_i:X\dasharrow X_i$ is the induced rational map and
if $\phi _i:X_i\to S_i$ are the corresponding 
contraction morphisms and $C_i=(h_i)_*C$, $H_i=(h_i)_*H$,
then\begin{enumerate}
\item $p_i:W\dasharrow X_i$ extracts no divisors,
\item there exist a $\Q$-divisor $B_{X_i}$ on $X_i$ and 
positive rational numbers $1=c_0\geq c_{1}\geq c_2\geq \ldots \geq c_N=0$ and 
$0=h_0\leq h_1\leq h_{2}\leq \ldots$
such that
$K_{X_i}+B_{X_i}+c_iC_i+h_iH_i$ is nef and $\phi _i$-numerically trivial,
\item $(X_i,B_{X_i}+c_iC_i+h_iH_i)\leq (W,B_{W}+c_iC_W+h_iH_W)$ cf. \eqref{d-1}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{claim}
\begin{remark}\label{r-1} We have that: \begin{enumerate}\item By \eqref{l-2}, $ (X_i,B_{X_i}+c_iC_i+h_iH_i)$
is kawamata log terminal.
\item Let $\Sigma _i$ be a very general $\phi _i$-vertical curve.
By our choices of $H$ and $C$, we have that 
$H_i\cdot \Sigma _i>0$ and $C_i\cdot \Sigma _i>0$ for all $i\geq 0$.
\item We have $h_i\leq 1$. If in fact $h_i>1$, then for $0<\epsilon \ll 1$,

$$K_W+B_W+(1+\epsilon)H_W=q^*(K_{X'}+B_{X'}+(1+\epsilon)H')+E'$$
where (by our choice of $H'$), $E'$ is effective and $q$-exceptional.

$$K_W+B_W+c_iC_W+h_iH_W=p_i^*(K_{X_i}+B_{X_i}+c_iC_i+h_iH_i)+E_i$$ where $E_i$
is effective and $p_i$-exceptional.
Since $\Sigma _i$ is a very general $\phi _i$ vertical curve. Then $\Sigma _i$
avoids the image of the exceptional locus of $p_i$ and so we may identify

Then, we have

will be constructed via a sequence of $K_{X_i}+B_i+c_iC_i+h_iH_i$-trivial

\begin{equation}\label{e-a}
(X_{i+1},B_{X_{i+1}}+c_iC_{i+1}+h_iH_{i+1})\leq (W,B_{W}+c_iC_W+h_iH_W),

c_iC_{i+1}+(h_i-\epsilon)H_{i+1}$ Mori fiber space if $h_i>0$.
\end{enumerate}\end{remark}

\begin{claim}\label{c-3}There are only finitely many possibilities for $\phi _i:X\to S_i$.\end{claim}
\begin{proof} We begin by showing that there are only finitely many possibilities for $X_i$.

Notice that the $(X_i,B_{X_i}+c_iC_i+h_iH_i)$ are minimal models for
$(W,B_W+c_iC_W+h_iH_W)$ where $0\leq c_i,h_i \leq 1$.
Suppose that $h_i=0$ for all $i$, then $s_i=h_{i+1}-h_i=0$ for all $i$
and so $c_i=c_0>0$ for all $i$ and by finiteness of models the claim follows.
 
If $h_i>0$ for some $i$, then $h_j\geq h_i$ for all $j\geq i$
and again by finiteness of models the claim follows.

The finiteness of the maps $\phi$ now follows as for each $X_i$ there are finitely many negative extremal rays.
\end{proof}
\begin{claim}The sequence of links $X_i\dasharrow X_{i+1}$ is finite.\end{claim}
\begin{proof} Assume that the given sequence of links is infinite.
By \eqref{c-3}, we may assume that $\phi _i:X_i\to S_i$ is isomorphic to $\phi _k:X_k\to S_k$ for some $i<k$. It then follows easily that
$c_{k+1}=c_{i+1}$ and $h_{k+1}=h_{i+1}$ and so $c_i=c_j$ for all $j\geq i$. 
Similarly, we have that $r_i=r_j$ for all $j\geq i$.

Assume that we have a link of type III or IV, then there are no $X$-exceptional divisors $E\subset W$ for which 
 $$a(E,X_i,B_{X_i}+c'_{i+1}C_i+h'_{i+1}H_i)< 
a(E,W,B_W+c'_{i+1}C_W+h'_{i+1}H_W).$$
As we have seen, (since $c_{i+1}=c_i$ and $r_{i+1}=r_i$), this property
continues to hold on $X_{i+1}$. But then all links are of type III or IV.
Since links of type III (resp. IV) decrease (do not change) $\rho (X_i)$,
there are only links of type IV.
Since links of type IVb (resp IVa) increase the discrepancies with respect 
to $K_X+B_X+c'C+h'H$ and strictly increase at least some discrepancy (resp.
do not change any discrepancy), there are no links of type IVb.
But for any link of type IVa, we have $r_{i+1}<r_i$ which is impossible.

Therefore, we may assume that we only have links of type I and II.
By considerations on $\rho (X_i)$, it follows that there are no links of type I. But links of type II increase the discrepancies with respect 
to $K_X+B_X+(c'-\delta)C+h'H$ (for $0<\delta \ll \epsilon \ll 1$)
and strictly increase at least some discrepancy.
This is the required contradiction.
\end{proof}
